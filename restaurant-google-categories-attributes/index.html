Google categories and attributes for restaurants: what to pick and where to edit

Your category tells Google what kind of business you are. Your attributes tell it, and your guests, what you offer. Both take about five minutes to check, and both can change without you. This guide covers the edit steps, the limits Google states, and what to do when a label is wrong.

How do I change my restaurant's category and attributes on Google?

Open your Business Profile and select Edit profile. For the category, select Edit next to "Business category", type a term, pick a suggestion and select Save. For attributes, select More, then Edit next to an attribute group, set each one to Yes or No, and save. Google says edits usually take about 10 minutes to review but can take up to 30 days.

Most owners set these once and never look again. Google says other people and Google itself can change parts of your profile later, so check each quarter.

Categories

What Google says about choosing a category

  • Pick the most specific option that is true. Google's own example is choosing "Nail salon" over "Salon". Use a general option only when no specific one fits.
  • You cannot invent a category. You choose from the list Google shows. You find options by typing a word into the box and reading the suggestions. The page describes no menu to browse.
  • Add a few additional categories, not one for every dish. Google says additional categories describe special departments or services, and it advises against adding one for every product or service.
  • Separate businesses get their own profile. If a department is independent, public-facing and separately owned and operated, Google says it should have its own profile, not a category on yours.

Google also says your categories affect your local ranking and can add a label to your business. It adds that the features available on your profile depend on your category. If a feature such as the menu editor is missing, check your primary category first.

The edit, in order

Six steps, checked against Google's help pages

Category steps from Manage your business category. Attribute steps from Manage your business attributes. Edit timing from Understand what happens to your Business Profile edits. More hours from Google's more hours page.

  1. Open the category box

    Go to your Business Profile and select Edit profile. Next to "Business category", select Edit. Google says full editing needs a verified profile.

  2. Set the primary category

    In the "Primary category" box, type a term that a guest would use to describe you, such as the cuisine or the format. Then select one option from the list that appears. Choose the option that is true on a normal Tuesday, not the one you hope to be.

  3. Add a small number of additional categories

    Select Add another category, type a term and select a suggestion. Add one only if it is a real part of the business. To remove one, select Remove next to it. Then select Save.

    Google says you "might be asked to verify your business again" after you add or edit a category, so it can confirm your information. If that happens, finish the verification. See Google Maps visibility for the wider profile checklist.

  4. Set your attributes

    Select Edit profile, then More. Next to the attribute group you want, select Edit. Next to each attribute, select Yes or No, then select Save. Google says some attributes can be changed only in certain locations, countries or categories, and that attribute names may change over time. Read the labels on your own screen.

  5. Check the status of your edit

    Google says edits usually take up to 10 minutes to review, and sometimes up to 30 days. A pending edit is still under review. An accepted edit appears on your profile. If Google cannot confirm an edit is accurate, it may not approve it, and you may be able to appeal. Use the "Check your edit status" link on Google's edit page.

  6. Set the hours that go with your service types

    If you offer delivery, takeout, drive through or pickup, Google lets you set separate hours for each. Select Edit profile, then Hours. Under "Add more hours", choose the type, set the days and hours and select Save. Google says these do not display until you set regular hours. See restaurant hours on Google for holidays.

Attributes

What attributes are, and which ones you control

Google describes attributes as details that show customers what your business offers. They appear on Search, Maps and other Google surfaces, and certain ones may help you appear in searches for places with that feature.

For restaurants, Google's Maps help describes a group of "services and features" that includes service options, dining options, amenities and payment options. We found no single Google page that lists every exact label. The labels you can set are the ones you see under More in your own profile, and Google says they vary by category.

Two groups of attributes are listed by name on Google's attribute page, and both can matter to a restaurant.

  • Accessibility. The listed names include "Wheelchair-accessible entrance", "Wheelchair-accessible restroom", "Wheelchair-accessible seating", "Wheelchair-accessible parking" and "Wheelchair-accessible elevator". Say Yes only if it is true.
  • Business identity. Google lists identity attributes such as "Women-owned", "Veteran-owned" and "Small business". You can opt in or out at any time. Google says franchises cannot be a small business.

Ordering and booking are separate from attributes. Google's restaurant help page links to its tools for online ordering, Reserve with Google and the menu editor. Our wrong menu guide covers the menu side.

When it changes without you

Who else can edit your profile

You are not the only source. Google's page on how it sources information lists public web content, licensed third-party data, user contributions and Google's own interactions with a business.

For attributes, Google states that some may be filled in from customer input, which means you cannot set them directly. Guests can also suggest edits in Maps. In the Maps app, a guest can open About, then Features, then Edit features, and mark an item True, False or Unsure. Google reviews these edits.

Google's page on updates adds a timeline. For some suggested edits you may get an email or an in-product notice before anything publishes. You then have 4 days to accept or reject. If you do not respond, Google may publish the edit when other public information, such as your website, supports it. You can edit your profile yourself at any time.

If your site and your profile disagree, you leave Google room to side with a guest's suggestion. Keep your site's structured data and your profile saying the same thing. Google does not say whether it can change categories or attributes on its own, so check them on a schedule instead of assuming they stay put.

Questions

Restaurant category and attribute questions

How many additional categories can a restaurant add?

Google's category help page does not state a number. It says to add a few for special departments or services, and advises against one for every product.

How long does a category or attribute change take?

Google says edits usually take about 10 minutes to review, but it can take up to 30 days. The category page gives no separate timeline.

Why did an attribute on my listing change when I did not touch it?

Google says some attributes are filled in from customer input, and that it may update a profile when sources such as user reports suggest the information is wrong. Guests can also suggest edits in Maps. Look for alerts under Edit profile, then correct anything wrong.

Can I type my own category if none fits?

No. Google says you cannot create your own category. If no specific option fits, Google says to use a general one.

Will I need to verify again after changing a category?

You might. Google says that after you add or edit a category you may be asked to verify your business again so it can confirm your information is accurate.
