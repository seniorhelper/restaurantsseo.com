Wrong menu, price or photo on Google: find the source and fix it

A guest says the price on Google is wrong. Or the menu photo is from 2021. Editing one place rarely fixes it, because your menu usually lives in several. This guide shows you how to find every copy, fix them in the right order, and check that the fix held.

Published November 23, 2026 Last checked November 2026 ~7 min read

How do I fix a wrong menu or price on Google?

Sign in to your Business Profile, choose Edit menu, change the item, and select Save. Google says changes can take 24 to 48 hours to show on Search and Maps. If the old data comes back, Google may be showing a different menu source. Open the Full menu tab, pick the source you want, and fix the other copies, such as your website, your PDF and your delivery apps, so they stop contradicting it.

Most fixes fail for one reason. The owner corrects the number in one place and never looks at the other four. This page follows the order that works: your own website first, then Google, then the apps.

Step zero

Find every place the menu lives

Before you edit anything, list every place a guest can see your menu. Search your restaurant name on a phone, in Google Maps and on each delivery app you use. Write down what each one shows and the date. It takes ten minutes, and it tells you which copy is the stale one.

Check these six places:

  • Your website menu page. Is it text or a PDF? Is the date or price old?
  • The Google menu. In Maps, tap your listing, then Menu.
  • The menu photos on your profile. Some are yours. Some were uploaded by guests.
  • The menu link on your profile. Does it open the current menu or an old file?
  • Each delivery app. DoorDash, Uber Eats, Grubhub and any others.
  • Your point-of-sale (POS) system. If it feeds an app, the app copy is controlled here.

Delivery apps may charge more than your dine-in menu on purpose. That is a pricing choice, not an error, but guests still notice. See restaurant menu SEO for why a clean text menu on your site comes first.

The fix, in order

Six steps, checked against Google's help pages

Menu editor steps from Google Business Profile Help: menu. Photo steps from Google's photo and video report page. Last checked November 2026. Google changes its interface often, so if a label differs, use the help page.

  1. Fix your own website first

    Your website is the copy you control completely, and other copies may point to it. Replace the PDF or image with a text menu, correct the prices, and keep the same page address. If your menu is only a photo, Google has to guess at the words. See how to build a readable menu page.

  2. Correct the items and prices in the Google menu editor

    Go to business.google.com and open your profile. Above the search results, select Edit menu. Select the item or section name. To add an item, select Add menu item, fill in the details and select Save. To change an item, edit it and select Save. To remove a dish, select Delete at the bottom of the item. Deleting a section also deletes every item in it.

    Google shows "Your menu has been updated. The updates will be published shortly." If you see "Something went wrong", try again, wait 24 hours, then contact support if it persists. The editor is for food and drink businesses. If it is missing, check your primary category and whether a provider supplies your menu through the Business Profile API.

  3. Choose the preferred source if Google lists more than one

    When Google finds several menus for your business, the Full menu tab inside Edit menu lets you pick one. Select your preferred source and select Save. Picking a source does not correct the others. The old copy still exists until you fix it where it lives.

  4. Replace old menu photos and the menu link

    Under Edit menu, open Photos of menu. Delete outdated files and upload the current one with Select photos or a PDF. Then go to Edit profile, Business information, Contact, Menu link, and enter the address of your current menu page. Link to the text page, not to a PDF.

    The Generate a menu option turns a photo into editable items. Google calls it experimental, and the whole menu must fit on one page in one photo. Check every price before you publish.

  5. Report a wrong or outdated photo uploaded by someone else

    On Google Search, open your profile, select Photos, then View all photos, pick the photo and select Report a problem. Choose a reason and select Submit. In the Maps app, open the photo, tap More, then Report. Google says a report is reviewed and the photo "may possibly be removed". Review can take several business days, and repeated reports on the same photo may slow it down. Report once, then upload a current photo of your own so the old one is not the first thing guests see.

  6. Update each delivery app, or the POS that feeds it

    On DoorDash, open Menu Manager, go to the Overview tab, select the pencil beside the item, change the price and select Save Changes, per DoorDash's help page. Menus synced from a POS must be changed in the POS, and DoorDash says POS changes can take up to 24 hours to appear.

    On Uber Eats, sign in to restaurant.uber.com, select Menu, pick the item, change the price and select Save, per Uber's help page. Uber says edits go live in the app right away, except photos, which are reviewed first and can take up to 72 hours. If your POS is integrated, change prices there instead.

  7. Wait, then check on a phone you do not use for work

    Allow 24 to 48 hours for Google. Then search your restaurant name on a phone that is not signed in to your business account, open Maps and tap Menu. Compare it to your list from step zero. Note any copy that still disagrees and go back to the place it lives.

One option to avoid: Google's Search Console has a setting called "Opt out of display on local search results and other Google properties". It works per domain, covers Shopping, Flights and Hotels as well as local results, and removes content within 30 days, per Google's help page. For a restaurant it is almost never the right move, because it also removes your site from local results. Fix the menu on your site instead.

Stop it coming back

Keep one source of truth

Wrong prices return when the menu is edited in six places by three people. Pick one master copy, usually your website page, and update the rest from it on the same day.

  • Assign one person to change prices, and change every copy in one sitting.
  • Put a date on the website menu. "Prices as of November 2026" tells guests and staff the copy is live.
  • Check your hours at the same time. Wrong hours and wrong prices come from the same neglect. See restaurant hours on Google.
Questions

Wrong menu and price questions

Why does Google show an old menu for my restaurant?

Google may find more than one menu for your business, and it can show one that is out of date. Open Edit menu in your Business Profile, go to the Full menu tab and choose your preferred source. That choice does not change the other copies, so also update your website, menu photos and delivery apps. Google says changes can take 24 to 48 hours to appear.

How long does it take for a menu change to show on Google?

Google's help page says changes can take 24 to 48 hours to display on Google Maps and Search. Check again after two days from a phone that is not signed in to your business account. If the old menu is still there, look for a second menu source on the Full menu tab or an old file in Photos of menu.

How do I remove a wrong menu photo someone else uploaded?

Open your profile on Google Search, choose Photos, then View all photos, select the photo and choose Report a problem. In the Maps app, open the photo, tap More, then Report. Google says the photo may possibly be removed after review, which can take several business days. Removal is not guaranteed.

Why are my delivery app prices different from my dine-in menu?

Many restaurants set higher prices on delivery apps to cover commission fees, and that is allowed. If the difference is a mistake, fix it in the app menu manager, or in your POS if the app syncs from it. If it is on purpose, say so on your website so guests are not surprised.

Should I opt my website out of Google local results to fix a wrong menu?

Almost never. The Search Console opt-out applies to your whole domain and removes your content from local search results, Shopping, Flights and Hotels within 30 days, according to Google. It is meant for site owners who want out, not for fixing a typo. Correct the menu on your website and in your Business Profile instead.
